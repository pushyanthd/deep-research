# Best 65-inch 2024 OLED for a dim-room movie-first buyer who also games on PS5

## Executive summary

Inference: The LG C4 is the best overall fit for this exact use mix because it pairs the strongest dim-room black-level/contrast evidence with the broadest gaming feature set: Dolby Vision, HDR10, HLG, four full-bandwidth HDMI 2.1 ports, 4K/120, 144Hz, VRR, ALLM, and Dolby Vision gaming. [S1] [S2]

Inference: The Samsung S90D is the brighter HDR alternative and RTINGS says it is mostly better than the C4 for HDR brightness, Game Mode brightness retention, color gamut, color volume, and HDR gradient handling; the trade-off is that it does not support Dolby Vision. [S2] [S3]

Inference: The Sony BRAVIA 8 is the motion-and-UI specialist, with strong motion clarity and Google TV, but it is the least flexible choice for a soundbar-plus-multiple-source setup because one of its two HDMI 2.1 ports is shared with eARC. [S4] [S5]

Inference: Value is only directional because the available prices are not a like-for-like live comparison: LG and Samsung both list $2,699.99 on their U.S. pages, while Abt lists the Sony at $1,998. [S6] [S7] [S8]

## Verdict and recommendation

Inference: Buy the LG C4 if your priority is dim-room movies with PS5 as a secondary use case, because it combines excellent contrast/black-level performance with the most complete gaming support of the three. [S1] [S2]

Inference: Buy the Samsung S90D instead if you care more about HDR punch than Dolby Vision; RTINGS says it is mostly better than the C4 for HDR brightness, HDR brightness retention in Game Mode, color gamut, color volume, and HDR gradient handling. [S2] [S3]

Inference: Buy the Sony BRAVIA 8 only if Sony’s motion style and Google TV matter enough to accept its HDMI trade-off; it has strong motion clarity and an easy interface, but one high-bandwidth port is consumed by eARC. [S4] [S5] [S4]

Inference: The practical trade-off is straightforward: LG is the safest movie-first balance, Samsung is the brightest HDR choice, and Sony is the motion/processing pick with the tightest HDMI headroom. [S1] [S3] [S4] [S2]

## Picture quality and HDR trade-offs

Inference: For dim-room movie viewing, the LG C4 is the most confidence-inspiring contrast-first option, because the review describes flawless black levels and perfect contrast/black detail in test scenes. [S1]

Inference: The Samsung S90D is the HDR-pop alternative: RTINGS says it is brighter in HDR, retains HDR brightness better in Game Mode, and has wider color gamut, better color volume, and better HDR gradient handling than the C4. [S2]

Inference: The main format trade-off is Dolby Vision versus Samsung’s brighter HDR presentation; the C4 keeps Dolby Vision, HDR10, and HLG support, while the S90D does not support Dolby Vision. [S1] [S2]

## PS5 gaming and HDMI limitations

Inference: The LG C4 is the most gaming-flexible choice because it has four full-bandwidth HDMI 2.1 ports and supports 4K/120, 144Hz, VRR, ALLM, FreeSync, G-SYNC Compatible, and Dolby Vision gaming in the supplied evidence. [S1] [S2]

Inference: The Samsung S90D is also fully PS5-ready with four HDMI 2.1 inputs, 4K/120, VRR, and ALLM, but it gives up Dolby Vision gaming. [S3] [S2]

Inference: The Sony BRAVIA 8 is the least convenient for a PS5-plus-soundbar-plus-other-sources setup because only HDMI ports 3 and 4 carry HDMI 2.1 bandwidth, and port 3 is also the eARC port, so you lose one high-bandwidth connection when you add a soundbar. [S4]

Inference: For a single PS5 the three sets all work; for a fuller game-room or AV stack, the C4 is the cleanest long-term fit. [S1] [S3] [S4] [S2]

## Motion, sports, and streaming performance

Inference: For processed video and streaming, the Samsung S90D has the strongest overall case in the supplied evidence, with exceptional clarity on difficult patterns, very clean low-bitrate smoothing, and only minor motion issues in challenging pans. [S3]

Inference: The LG C4 is still very strong for sports and fast motion thanks to its 144Hz panel, motion-smoothing controls, and sub-1 ms response time, but its tonal transitions are the weaker part of its processing story. [S1]

Inference: The Sony BRAVIA 8 delivers the cleanest motion-clarity profile on paper, with no micro-judder and very clear motion, but RTINGS still describes its upscaling and color accuracy as weaker than the C4’s overall image processing. [S4] [S2]

Inference: Samsung’s Tizen and LG’s webOS both look mature on paper, with Samsung emphasizing broad app support and Gaming Hub, while LG’s smart-platform package is presented as polished and mainstream; for buyers using a soundbar, software polish matters more here than the unresolved speaker ranking.
